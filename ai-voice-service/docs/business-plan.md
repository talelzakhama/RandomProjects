# Business plan (DRAFT v0.1)

## Hypothesis
Small home-service businesses lose qualified leads when they cannot answer calls. A reliable, clearly disclosed AI phone receptionist could capture these leads and send actionable summaries.

## Initial focus
- Geography: Los Angeles.
- Niche: plumbing or another home-service vertical selected after interviews.
- Buyer: owner/operator of a small business with missed calls or after-hours demand.
- Offering: setup and configuration + monthly managed support. Customer usage may be metered separately.
- Differentiator: measurable reliability, realistic voice behavior, business-specific integration and QA.

## Discovery before code expansion
Interview 10 businesses; ask volume of missed calls, current workflow, average job value, seasonality, current phone/CRM setup, budget, consent requirements, and willingness to pilot. Avoid leading questions. Aim for 2–3 credible pilot candidates; this is a target, not validation already achieved.

## Illustrative pricing hypotheses (NOT quoted market research)
- Implementation: $500–$2,000 depending on workflow.
- Support/management: $200–$600/month, PLUS usage or an included-minute tier with overages.
- Actual pricing must account for call minutes, telecom charges, model/tool costs, taxes, support labor, refunds and customer acquisition.
- Do not promise cost savings or recovered revenue without measured evidence.

## Go-to-market
1. Interview businesses and define one buyer profile.
2. Build a short demo with realistic scenarios.
3. Outreach through existing connections and direct local prospecting (not bulk unsolicited calls).
4. Offer narrowly scoped, paid or carefully defined pilot with written terms.
5. Capture ROI proxies: answered calls, qualified leads, handoff rate, appointments later confirmed, cost per call and retention.

## Risks & mitigations
- Wrong answers: strict approved knowledge base, escalation, abstention.
- Emergency calls: clear routing to human/emergency instructions; no dispatch promises.
- Privacy/recording rules: legal review of notices, recording/consent, data agreements and retention before pilots.
- Outages: human fallback and alerting.
- Vendor lock-in: provider adapter and portable business logic.
- Cross-border expansion: revisit local languages, phone routing, tax, entity, privacy and telecom rules; do not assume a US entity alone solves compliance.

## Current unknowns
Preferred niche; interviews; Vapi vs Retell; CRM integration; hosting; legal contracts/insurance; actual pricing; jurisdiction-specific rules; LLC timing and entity needs.
